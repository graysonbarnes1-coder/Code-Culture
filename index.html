Simón Bolívar

Simón Bolívar, also known as “the Liberator”, freed 6 countries from Spanish rule. He was born to a wealthy landowner in Venezuela and eventually got education in Europe where he learned about Enlightenment ideals. These ideals prioritized people having their own choices and being less reliant on rulers. This inspired Bolívar to fight multiple wars of independence in South America while Napoleon of France was fighting Spain. This opening allowed Bolívar to help gain independence for present-day Venezuela, New Granada (Colombia), Ecuador, Peru, Bolivia, and Panama.

Simón Bolívar also greatly shaped the countries that he liberated. The most obvious effects are in the names of countries such as the Bolivarian Republic of Venezuela and Bolivia. While he failed with the unification of South America with Gran Colombia, a unified state of Venezuela, Ecuador, and New Granada when it dissolved in 1831, he inspired a sense of Pan-Americanism, or South American unity. He also has multiple monuments to him throughout countries he liberated and has currencies named after him, the Bolívar and Boliviano. His very personality has been infused into many South American cultures leading to him being seen as the greatest hero or a mythological figure. Towards the end of his life and to now, there has been controversy about his methods and whether he really encompassed his ideas or if he was too authoritarian. Despite this, he is still a prominent figure representing South American independence and nationalism.
